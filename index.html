Japanese investment, by state

Documented commitments and transactions, 2014–29 Oct 2026. Not FDI stock.

Arrow keys move between states. Enter opens the records behind the focused state. Escape closes the detail.

Notes

Methodology

Documented investment commitments and transactions involving Japanese firms, 2014 through 29 October 2026. The $41.036 billion on the map is that documented subtotal.

How the records were built

Six regional research agents searched company releases, filings, and government records. They looked for strong primary-source examples in each state. The ledger holds 67 records. Forty-seven have a state-specific amount. Twenty are undisclosed or not allocated to a state. This is not a census of every Japanese investment in the United States.

Each record names a Japanese parent, a U.S. place, the amount when a source states one, the year, the measurement basis, and the status. Origin points are approximate parent-office cities. U.S. points are approximate project cities, or a company headquarters when that is what the source gives. If the city is unresolved, the map uses the state center.

What a state total means

The color of a state is the sum of its documented, state-specific amounts, each record counted once. A later project total replaces earlier nested announcements for the same scope. A global acquisition price is not given to the headquarters state. A partner’s separate contribution is not added again when it is already inside a whole project budget.

North Carolina is the arithmetic in plain sight: Toyota’s $13.9 billion campus scope plus Fujifilm’s $3.2 billion campus scope is the $17.1 billion on the map. That is not verified completed expenditure, and it is not the state’s foreign-direct-investment stock.

Forty-one states have a number. Ten have a record and no state total. The map leaves those hatched. A blank is not zero, and no record is not evidence of no investment.

Announced, completed, and acquired

The $41.036 billion mixes three measures. $36.090 billion is announced project or financing commitments. $3.708 billion is reported completed project value. $1.238 billion is acquisition or equity consideration. An announcement is not money spent. An acquisition price is not new construction. Figures are nominal, not inflation-adjusted, and they are not a stock of investment still in place.

What this is not

These figures are not FDI stock and not verified bank transfers from Japan. A Japanese owner can pay with U.S. borrowing, retained earnings, or a partner’s funds. Ownership is not financing origin. Some amounts are whole project budgets shared with other companies. Louisiana’s $4 billion Blue Point figure is the joint venture, not Mitsui’s share alone.

An arc links a parent office to a U.S. project or company reference. It shows a corporate connection, not the path the money traveled.

Funding by company

Firm groups follow the ledger. Each documented amount is counted once. Records with no state amount are listed and left undisclosed.

About these figures

These totals add selected, sourced amounts involving Japanese firms from 2014 through 29 October 2026. The search looked for strong primary-source examples. It is not a census of every investment.

The $41.036 billion is a documented subtotal. It is not Japan’s cash contribution, not state foreign-direct-investment stock, and not a verified transfer from a Japanese bank. Figures are nominal, not inflation-adjusted.

Color is that state subtotal. A blank amount stays undisclosed — it is not zero. Ten states have a record and no state total. Announced commitments, completed projects, and acquisitions are different measures; each record keeps its own.

Some amounts are whole project budgets shared with partners. An arc links a parent office to a U.S. project, headquarters, or state reference. It is a corporate link, not a financing route.
